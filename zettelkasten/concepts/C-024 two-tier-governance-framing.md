The two governance sources in the income conflict are graded, not equated. **eIDAS ARF is a hard
requirement**: a legal mandate fixing the format family. **GDPR data minimization is operationally
binding**: not a textual format mandate, but a principle the paper operationalizes as a predicate-proof
requirement, backed by an enforcement precedent. Grading them pre-empts the reviewer objection that the
conflict is manufactured by over-reading GDPR: even under a conservative reading, the operational
interpretation is defensible, and the conflict is real.

This framing matters for the necessity argument: the value of multi-layer modeling is that it
preserves *which* source imposes *which* requirement, so a conflict can be located rather than dissolved.
