# Proto-SLR Axis (b): EUDI Wallet: ARF, Large-Scale Pilots, Member-State Wallets

## Scope

This axis covers the European Digital Identity Wallet as the paper's running instantiation of a VC ecosystem under construction: the Architecture and Reference Framework (ARF) as the normative architecture, the scholarly and expert critique of that architecture (privacy, cryptography, protocol security, threat modeling), the legal reading of the wallet under Regulation (EU) 2024/1183, member-state implementation perspectives (Italy, Finland), and the pre-deployment empirical evidence around the large-scale pilots and rollout. The paper needs this axis because the EU eIDAS 2 / EUDI Wallet / GDPR stack is the concrete system whose multi-source governance constraints the three-layer metamodel formalizes (RQ1), and because the ARF's documented pre-deployment critique record is the strongest available evidence that VC-ecosystem design errors are visible in specifications before deployment (RQ0), which is the load-bearing premise for anticipating errors in the not-yet-existing B2B/B2G VC ecosystems.

## Classification

- **ARF v2.x (standard-spec): must-cite.** The normative anchor of the entire axis; the metamodel's roles and requirements (RQ1) and the version-churn evidence for requirement instability (RQ0) are unarguable without it.
- **Abellan Alvarez, Hoelzmer, Sedlmeir 2026 (Computers & Security): must-cite.** The central peer-reviewed privacy analysis of the ARF; its documented pre-deployment linkability and over-disclosure failures are the strongest single piece of RQ0 anticipatability evidence.
- **Cryptographers' Feedback 2024: must-cite.** The canonical documented case of the research community flagging a design error (batch-issuance pseudo-unlinkability) before deployment; RQ0 cannot be argued without it.
- **Slamanig 2025 (arXiv): must-cite.** The scholarly articulation of the theory-practice adoption gap as a recurring, predictable failure pattern; it carries the error-history side of the RQ0 argument and links the EUDI episode to the longer identity-ecosystem record.
- **Ebadi Ansaroudi et al. 2025 (EURASIP JIS): must-cite.** The only item quantifying a regulation-vs-deployment-reality conflict (roughly 10.5 percent of Italian-market phones meet the WSCD bar); direct evidence that governance constraints are checkable against the deployment spectrum, bridging RQ0 and RQ1.
- **Termont, Esteves 2026 (arXiv): must-cite.** The only item interrogating the trusted-list governance layer and the deprecated-flow inheritance in the OpenID stack; it supplies a governance-layer error class for the taxonomy that no other item covers. Preprint status should be flagged when cited.
- **Inza 2025 (Springer LGTS): must-cite.** The only legal-scholarship treatment in the corpus of the wallet's roles and obligations under Regulation (EU) 2024/1183; the metamodel's regulatory layer needs a scholarly anchor beyond the primary legal text.
- **NiScy study 2026 (EC report): must-cite.** The only cross-EU demand-side evidence; it substantiates the RQ0 premise that even the B2C ecosystem is nascent and demand evidence exists only as pre-deployment surveys. Grey literature; cite as such.
- **Sharif et al. 2025 (CRiSIS): differentiate.** Closest peer-reviewed precedent to the paper's design-error taxonomy; must be explicitly distinguished as threat-level and single-ecosystem versus the paper's design-error-level, cross-spectrum, formally grounded taxonomy.
- **Hauck 2023 (thesis): differentiate.** The closest existing formal analysis of the EUDI protocol stack; the paper must state its delta explicitly (protocol-level WIM analysis versus ecosystem/governance-level graph-predicate metamodel).
- **De Marco, Marino, De Maria 2024 (CEUR-WS): differentiate.** An informal legal-requirement-to-technical-architecture mapping by IT-Wallet architects; it is the informal predecessor of exactly what RQ1 formalizes and needs a one-line distinction.
- **Sitouah, Esposito, Bruschi 2026 (arXiv): differentiate.** Contains a preliminary SLR over SSI-eIDAS alignment; a reviewer could ask how this proto-SLR relates to it, so the coverage relationship must be stated explicitly.
- **Saifullah et al. 2026 (NCDHWS): differentiate.** Independently converges on a three-layer ecosystem framing (citizen, wallet, governance); the superficial similarity to the paper's three-layer metamodel needs a one-line distinction (behavioral adoption framework versus structural formal metamodel).

Counts: must-cite 8, differentiate 5, ignore 0.

## What exists, what is thin

The corpus is dense exactly where the ARF is the object of critique: privacy analysis (Abellan Alvarez et al.), cryptographic adequacy (Cryptographers' Feedback, Slamanig), protocol security (Hauck), architecture (Termont and Esteves), threat modeling (Sharif et al.), and hardware feasibility (Ebadi Ansaroudi et al.). This gives the paper an unusually well-documented record of pre-deployment design criticism for a single ecosystem, spanning peer-reviewed venues, expert statements, and preprints, plus a legal-scholarship reading (Inza) and a practitioner legal-to-technical mapping (De Marco et al.). Note the recency skew: five of thirteen items are from 2026 and three are preprints, so several classifications rest on work that has not completed peer review.

The axis is thin in three places, and this should be stated in the paper rather than papered over. First, the large-scale pilots (POTENTIAL, EWC, DC4EU, NOBID) have no scholarly evaluation in this corpus at all; the only empirical pre-rollout evidence found is the NiScy preference survey, a contractor-produced EC report. Second, member-state wallet coverage is effectively two countries: Italy (De Marco et al., Ebadi Ansaroudi et al.) and a Finland-context adoption review (Saifullah et al.); there is nothing on the German, Spanish, or other national wallet efforts. Third, there is no post-deployment evidence of any kind, which is expected given the 2026 rollout timeline but means every RQ0 claim from this axis is about errors visible in specifications and pilots, not errors observed in operation.

## RQ0 evidence

No item in this axis characterizes design errors of future VC-based B2B/B2G ecosystems, and no item defines a general VC-ecosystem concept of the kind RQ0 requires. That gap is the paper's own contribution and should be claimed as such. What the axis does provide is the B2C half of the anticipatability argument, with precise pointers:

- **Errors visible pre-deployment in the specification.** Abellan Alvarez, Hoelzmer, Sedlmeir (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cose.2025.104707) map information flows in three ARF use cases and argue the EUDIW as specified fails unlinkability, documenting linkability, identifiability, and over-disclosure risks in the specification before any deployment. The Cryptographers' Feedback (HPI PDF locator above) records sixteen cryptographers concluding that ARF 1.4's batch-issuance approach to unlinkability is inadequate and that a redesign toward BBS-family anonymous credentials is needed. Hauck (https://doi.org/10.18419/opus-13772) finds phishing attacks on the OID4VCI pre-authorized code flow and the cross-device flow by formal analysis of the spec. These are the concrete documented instances behind the claim that VC-ecosystem design errors are anticipatable from current specifications.
- **Errors as a recurring, predictable pattern.** Slamanig (https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07209) frames the non-adoption of decades-old anonymous-credential cryptography as a recurring failure pattern in identity ecosystems, using the EUDI episode as the running case; this is the closest any item comes to the paper's error-history thesis, though its history is the anonymous-credentials literature, not X.509/PKI.
- **Ecosystem definition, partially.** The ARF (standard-spec item) defines the ecosystem's roles (PID provider, wallet provider, relying party, trusted lists) normatively, and Inza (https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-74889-9_19) defines its legal existence conditions under Regulation (EU) 2024/1183. Both define the EUDI B2C ecosystem specifically, not the general VC-ecosystem concept, and neither extends to B2B/B2G.

Caveat: the pointers above paraphrase findings as confirmed during corpus verification; exact quotations should be pulled from the sources at drafting time, not from this file.

Verdict: the axis strongly evidences that design errors are anticipatable pre-deployment in the B2C instantiation, but the extension of that claim to hypothetical B2B/B2G VC ecosystems is unsupported by any item here and must be argued by the paper itself (with axis coverage of X.509/PKI error history coming from another axis).

## RQ1 anchors

- **ARF v2.x** is the primary anchor: it is the artifact whose roles, trust relationships, and requirements the metamodel's architecture layer must reproduce, and whose version churn (2.4.0 hosted docs; releases referencing 2.6.0 and 2.7.3) motivates machine-checkable constraints over hand-maintained conformance.
- **De Marco, Marino, De Maria 2024** is the informal precedent: an explicit legal-requirement-to-technical-solution mapping by member-state implementers, which is precisely the mapping RQ1 asks whether one formal metamodel can capture; the paper's delta is formalization and cross-source consistency checking.
- **Hauck 2023** is the formal-methods neighbor: it shows formal modeling of the EUDI credential-exchange protocols is feasible and productive, but at protocol level in the Web Infrastructure Model; the paper's delta is the ecosystem/governance level and graph-predicate (Refinery) machinery.
- **Abellan Alvarez, Hoelzmer, Sedlmeir 2026** contributes a systematic mapping of roles and information flows across the ARF that the metamodel's cross-layer constraints can be validated against.
- **Ebadi Ansaroudi et al. 2025** anchors the claim that governance constraints (WSCD certification requirements) can be checked against deployment-spectrum facts (device capabilities), which is the kind of cross-layer predicate the metamodel is built to express.
- **Inza 2025** anchors the regulatory layer: the legal roles, obligations, and trust services the metamodel's top layer must represent.
