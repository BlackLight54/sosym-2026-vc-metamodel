0.Decisions & framing

Four framing decisions the paper must commit to before the EU-regulatory material is drafted. Each states the options, the recommended choice, and what that choice binds the paper to. The recommended one-paragraph framing at the end synthesizes all four.

A Is verifier-side (relying-party) governance in scope, out of scope, or an extension axis?
  • Issuer-side remit, name the verifier extension recommend — scope the paper to issuer-side credential design and name verifier-side governance (RP registration under CIR 2025/848) as the adjacent extension of the same cross-layer-constraint method, cited as breadth evidence that multi-source governance is pervasive.
  • Silent on the verifier axis (current draft) adversarial reviewer: "you model half the governance"
  • Retrofit the verifier axis under deadline — a missing entity class and constraint family, not a tweak
Recommend: issuer-side remit, verifier axis named as the extension. Naming the boundary converts a genuine gap into evidence for the approach: an over-asking check is structurally a cross-layer predicate (requested attributes against registered attributes), so the method carries over even though the current metamodel does not express it.
Commits the paper to: a §5.3 external-validity paragraph that names the issuer/verifier boundary and cites CIR 2025/848 as breadth evidence rather than leaving the verifier axis silent.
B Is the vertical conflict presented as regulator-documented or author-constructed?
  • Regulator-documented, the ARF's own text as primary evidence recommend — headline ARF v2.9.0 §7.4.3.5.1, which names Relying-Party linkability as a privacy risk and concedes the salted-hash baseline is linkable; the CSOK running example instantiates the same shape rather than originating it.
  • Author-constructed example first invites "is this a real problem?"
Recommend: regulator-documented. The strongest evidence is the framework's own admission, not our example or third-party critique. Leading with the regulator's text pre-empts the "constructed straw problem" objection and lets the in-regulation evidence carry the argument independently of the unverified academic sources.
Commits the paper to: quoting ARF §7.4.3.5.1 in the motivation and positioning CSOK as an instantiation of a live conflict, not its origin.
C Is the eIDAS expressiveness result (the highest-risk binding claim) framed as a strength or a limitation?
  • Strength first, own the partials recommend — all eight ARF constraints expressible (three fully, five partially, none outside the metamodel), then explain the five partials via their two named root causes.
  • Strength only — headline the result, leave the partials unexamined adversarial reviewer attacks the gap
  • Limitation first — undersells a genuinely positive result
Recommend: strength first, then own the partials. This is the claim most exposed to a rigorous reviewer. Stating plainly what "partial" means, and why, is more credible than a clean headline and disarms the objection before it is raised.
Commits the paper to: a short "what partial expressibility means and why" paragraph naming the two gaps (attestation-qualification levels; claim-granularity privacy annotations).

  • Dated snapshot, method over catalogue recommend — state an explicit as-of date (June 2026, ARF v2.9.0) and frame the eIDAS/GDPR/VCDM catalogue as a dated instantiation of a method (cross-layer constraints as predicates) that outlives any version.
  • Pin to one version silently — out of date by publication
Recommend: dated snapshot, method over catalogue. Foregrounding the durable method, with the regulation as a timestamped instance, protects the contribution from the regulation moving again between submission and print.

Recommended one-paragraph framing

A method for detecting cross-layer design errors in interrelated verifiable-credential schemas under multiple, independently-enacted governance sources (W3C VCDM, eIDAS 2 / ARF, GDPR). The motivation is a conflict the EU's own framework documents, not one we constructed; the eIDAS/GDPR/VCDM stack is a dated instantiation (June 2026, ARF v2.9.0) of a method that outlives it. The remit is issuer-side credential design; verifier-side governance (relying-party registration under CIR 2025/848) is the named, adjacent extension, evidence that multi-source governance is pervasive rather than a gap in the model.

Evidence base

1.Is the problem real? Yes, and the regulator documents it

The strongest evidence is the ARF's own text, not our constructed example or third-party critique. ARF v2.9.0 §7.4.3.5.1 names Relying-Party Linkability as a privacy risk and concedes the mandated salted-hash baseline is linkable:

"By comparing the received salt values, the Relying Party may find matching salt values ... conclude that the corresponding attestations must have been the same." EUDI Wallet ARF v2.9.0, §7.4.3.5.1 Linkability

Zero-knowledge proofs sit in a separate subsection (§7.4.3.5.3) as a not-yet-baseline mitigation. The governance source therefore documents the exact vertical conflict the paper formalizes: a statutory unlinkability duty against linkable mandated formats, with the fix acknowledged but not bound.

The mechanism, precisely

The conflict is not rhetorical. It follows from format capabilities the regulation itself fixes:

  1. Both mandated baseline formats, ISO/IEC 18013-5 mdoc (MSO) and SD-JWT VC, realize selective disclosure with salted-hash commitments. The holder can hide fields, but the disclosed salts are stable across presentations, so two verifiers (or a verifier and an issuer) can correlate them. This is disclosure-with-linkability, not zero-knowledge.
  2. Genuine unlinkability, and predicate proofs that establish "income > threshold" without revealing the value, require ZKP / multi-message-signature (BBS-type) schemes. In the EU stack these are TS13 and TS14: published, but out of baseline and bound by no implementing act.
  3. Dual issuance (CIR 2024/2977) requires a PID in both mdoc and SD-JWT VC. The conjunction removes the usual escape hatch of "pick the more capable format": both mandated formats share the same capability ceiling.
  4. Two statutory duties pull the other way: unlinkability where identification is not needed (Art. 5a(16)(b)) and request-time data minimization (Art. 5b(3)).

So the set {mandated, linkable, non-predicate baseline formats} together with {statutory unlinkability + minimization} is jointly unsatisfiable under the currently approved capability set. That is the paper's vertical Headline-1 conflict, occurring verbatim in the live framework. The CSOK running example instantiates the same shape, and its G0–G7 ablation shows that only the full conjunction of governance sources is unsatisfiable, every proper subset is satisfiable, so the conflict is structural, not an artifact of one over-read rule.

Supporting layers of evidence, weighted by strength:

  • In-force mechanism primary — Reg. (EU) 2024/1183 Art. 5a(16)(b) unlinkability duty; mandated baseline formats are linkable (salted-hash, not ZK); Art. 5b(3) data-minimization bound at registration; dual issuance of two formats (mdoc and SD-JWT VC), neither of which supports predicate proofs.
  • Technology exists but is not bound primary — unlinkability-capable schemes are now published EC specs (TS13, TS14) yet remain non-mandated. State the contradiction as "governance does not yet bind the available technology," not "technology missing."
  • Institutional corroboration primary — a national DPA (AEPD) treats minimization and linkability against the wallet as a live obligation.
  • Academic / civil-society unverified — cryptographers' feedback on the ARF; SoK papers (Biedermann, Schwalm). Corroborating, not load-bearing (see §5).

3.Threats to applicability, ranked by reviewer risk

  1. Relying-party scope gap highest
    The three layers (DCL/CSL/FSL) are issuer/credential-structure-centric. eIDAS 2 Art. 5b and CIR (EU) 2025/848 (adopted 6 May 2025, applicable 24 Dec 2026) impose a verifier-side regime the model does not express: register per intended use the attestations/attributes requested (Annex I point 9), a typed entitlement (Service_Provider, PID_Provider, QEAA_Provider, Non_Q_EAA_Provider, PUB_EAA_Provider), and the competent DPA; Art. 5b(3) forbids requesting beyond what was registered; over-asking grounds suspension (Art. 9(2)(c)); access is mediated by access certificates, not a Trusted List. Sibling instruments out of reach: cross-border matching (2025/846), breach notification (2025/847), certified-wallet listing (2025/849), trust/status governance. Double-edged: it strengthens the motivation and is a genuine limitation at the same time.
    Raised by: the adversarial reviewer ("you model half the governance") and the practitioner ("real deployments turn on who may ask"). Severity: high, it touches the central claim. Mitigation: Decision A, name the boundary and cite CIR 2025/848 as breadth evidence, not silence.
  2. Moving-target regulation
    The stack moved across three CIR batches between Dec 2024 and Aug 2025, ARF advanced two minor versions in six months, and CIR 2025/848 is in force but only applicable from 24 Dec 2026, after a typical mid-2026 submission. A reader checking the citations at review time may find them superseded.
    Raised by: the practitioner and the adversarial reviewer. Severity: medium, it threatens currency not correctness. Mitigation: Decision D, dated snapshot plus method-over-catalogue framing.
  3. Coarse format reading
    The paper's binary "SD-JWT-VC or mdoc" simplifies the current four-realization rule (ETSI TS 119 472-1 v1.2.1; ARF v2.9.0 §5.4). The simplification is harmless for the headline conflict (all baseline realizations share the salted-hash ceiling) but a precise reviewer will flag the imprecision.
    Raised by: the standards-literate reviewer. Severity: low. Mitigation: refine the wording per correction #2 without relaxing the qualified-attestation bar (refuted softening, 1-2).
  4. Single-jurisdiction (EU) framing
    The governance catalogue is EU-specific (eIDAS/GDPR/VCDM). The method is jurisdiction-agnostic, but the paper currently asserts rather than demonstrates that it transfers to, say, the US or a sectoral framework.
    Raised by: the generalizability reviewer. Severity: low-medium. Mitigation: claim only what is shown, position the EU stack as one instantiation of the method (Decision D), and name a second jurisdiction as future work.
  5. Tool reliance (Refinery)
    The constraints are expressed and solved in one framework. Whether they port to another partial-model finder or a generic SMT encoding is untested, so reproducibility depends on a single research prototype.
    Raised by: the formal-methods reviewer. Severity: low. Mitigation: argue the predicates are framework-independent (graph predicates over partial models) and flag portability as future work (Q-006).

4.Relying-party regime, in detail

Governance imposed on the verifier, structurally separate from issuer-side credential schemas and formats:

  • Instrument: CIR (EU) 2025/848, legal basis Art. 5b(11) of Reg. (EU) 910/2014 (Art. 5b inserted by Reg. (EU) 2024/1183). Adopted 6 May 2025, published 7 May 2025, applicable 24 Dec 2026.
  • What must be registered: for each intended use, the data (attestations and attributes) the RP intends to request (Annex I point 9); a typed entitlement; the competent DPA.
  • Minimization enforcement: Art. 5b(3), "Relying parties shall not request users to provide any data other than that indicated pursuant to paragraph 2, point (c)." Over-asking is a suspension ground (CIR 2025/848 Art. 9(2)(c)).
  • Trust mechanism: ARF v2.9.0 states there is no Trusted List or LoTE for relying parties; an RP holds access certificate(s) from an Access Certificate Authority.

Why the metamodel cannot express this, and what it would take

The three layers model the credential and its issuer-side format governance: DCL (domain claims), CSL (schemas, credentials, subjects), FSL (formats, capabilities, issuer governance annotations). The relying-party regime governs a different actor and a different act, the verifier's request. Expressing it would require model elements the metamodel does not have:

  • a Verifier / Relying-Party entity carrying its registered intended-uses and a typed entitlement;
  • a request relation: the attributes an RP may ask for, per intended use;
  • an access-policy predicate over that relation, with over-asking as the violation (request ⊄ registration);
  • an access-certificate artifact as the trust anchor, in place of the issuer-side trust assumption.

That is a missing entity class and a missing constraint family, not a tweak. It is also why Decision A matters: the clean move is to name this as the verifier-side extension of the same cross-layer-constraint method, not to retrofit it under deadline. Notably, the method itself would carry over, an over-asking check is structurally a cross-layer predicate (request attributes against registered attributes), which is evidence for, not against, the approach.

Imprecisions to carry forward
RP registration certificates were made optional (April 2025 comitology); cite the "registration record" for the mandatory backstop. The minimization duty attaches to Art. 5b(3), not to ETSI TS6. Entitlement types are set by CIR 2025/848 Annex I; ETSI TS 119 475 only defines their normative URIs.
Refuted — do not assert 0-3
Runtime wallet comparison of request-vs-registration as a settled, format-orthogonal enforcement mechanism is a discussion-topic proposal, not in force.
