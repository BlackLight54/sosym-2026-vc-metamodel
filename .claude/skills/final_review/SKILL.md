# Skill: Final Review (Orchestrator)

**Purpose:** Full-paper review before submission. This skill orchestrates other skills for mechanical checks and focuses itself on what only a sequential human-like read can catch: argument coherence, champion potential, and aesthetic quality.

## Trigger

- "Final review"
- "Polish pass"
- "Read the whole paper and tell me what's wrong"

## Prerequisites

Run these first. Final review assumes they pass:

1. **`skills/pre_submission_check`** — all markers resolved, refs intact, anonymity clean.
2. **`skills/claim_evidence_audit`** — all claims have proportional evidence, contribution map is current.

If either has outstanding issues, fix those before running final review. This skill does not re-check what they cover.

## Inputs

- All files in `sections/`, read in order.
- VENUE.md (reviewer calibration, champion test).
- CLAUDE.md (thesis — does the paper deliver?).
- Figures in `figures/`.

## Steps

### 1. Full sequential read

Read every section in order as a reviewer would. Track:

- **Argument thread:** Continuous logical chain from problem → gap → contribution → evidence → conclusion? Any breaks?
- **Promise tracking:** Every claim made early is a promise. Verify each is fulfilled. Flag unfulfilled promises.
- **Terminology drift:** Note every key term at first use. Flag meaning shifts or synonym creep.
- **Forward/backward references:** Does the intro structure paragraph match actual sections? Do "as we show in Section 4" references point to real content?

### 2. Argument coherence

For each section:

- **Earns its space?** Content that could be cut without weakening the argument? Content missing that the argument needs?
- **Doing one job?** If two things, should it be split?
- **Transition in?** Can a reader follow why this section comes after the previous?
- **Transition out?** Does the last paragraph set up what's next?

### 3. Aesthetic review

**Paragraph-level:**
- Paragraphs longer than ~8 sentences → split candidate.
- Single-sentence paragraphs → expand or merge.
- Weak openers ("It is worth noting...", "In this section, we...") → cut wrapper.
- First sentence of each paragraph: does it state the paragraph's point?

**Sentence-level:**
- Logically inert sentences.
- Hedge stacking.
- Unmotivated passive voice.
- Sentences over ~40 words.

**Figure/table integration:**
- Every figure referenced near where it appears?
- Text explains what to see, not just "see Figure X"?
- Captions self-contained?
- Tables with meaningful headers, referenced with interpretation?

**Notation and formatting:**
- Math notation consistent?
- Definition/theorem numbering consistent?
- Venue conventions followed?

### 4. Reviewer simulation

Adopt each of the four personas from VENUE.md (generated by `skills/reviewer_personas`, based on archetypes from `guides/reviewer_archetypes`):

**Champion:**
- What would they fight for in the PC meeting?
- Is the champion insight visible and compelling?
- What score and why?

**Mathematician:**
- What technical gaps?
- What missing related work?
- Are definitions precise and proofs convincing?

**Business Manager:**
- Where would they get confused?
- Does the significance argument land, or would they say "so what?"
- What would make them care?

**Adversarial:**
- What's the weakest link they'd attack?
- What competing approach would they advocate for instead?
- What would their "reject" argument be?

If reviewer personas haven't been generated yet, flag it — they should be generated before running final review.

### 5. Champion test

Run `skills/champion_test`. At the final review stage, this evaluates whether the full paper delivers a champion-worthy insight and whether that insight is visible in the abstract, introduction, and conclusion.

If the test fails at this stage, it's a delivery or presentation problem — the paper is written, so the fix is to foreground, reframe, or emphasize, not to change the contribution.

### 6. Abstract and title alignment

**Abstract:** Re-read each sentence. Does the paper deliver? Has the framing drifted? If diverged, flag for revision via `skills/abstract_scaffold` in revision mode.

**Title:** Does the title accurately set expectations for what the paper delivers? Would a reviewer reading only the title correctly predict the paper's contribution type? Is it specific enough to distinguish this paper from adjacent work?

### 7. Report

Present findings in three tiers:

**Critical (will cost review score points):**
- Argument gaps, unfulfilled promises, coherence breaks.
- Figures that don't match text.
- Champion test failure (no clear reason for a reviewer to fight for acceptance).

**Important (reviewer will note):**
- Aesthetic issues, weak transitions, redundant content.
- Missing figure interpretations.
- Terminology inconsistencies.
- Title–content misalignment.

**Polish (nice-to-fix):**
- Sentence-level improvements.
- Minor formatting.

For each finding: exact location, the problem, why it matters, and a suggested fix.

### 8. Generate revision prompts (if needed)

If critical or important findings require multi-section changes, run `skills/revision_orchestration` to produce ordered prompt files in `prompts/`.

### 9. Update TODO.md

Critical items as must-fix. Important items to queue. Polish as a single grouped task.

## Execution strategy

This is the most context-intensive skill. For long papers (>12 pages), split into two passes:

- **Pass 1:** Sections 1 through midpoint. Output: interim findings.
- **Pass 2:** Remaining sections + full-paper synthesis. Input: interim findings from pass 1.

Each pass produces its own report. Merge for the final output.

## Output

- Tiered report.
- Reviewer simulation results.
- Champion test assessment.
- Revision prompts in `prompts/` (if needed).
- TODO.md updated.
