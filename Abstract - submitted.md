Emerging digital credential ecosystems will allow persons and organizations to receive, hold, and selectively present cryptographically verifiable claims about themselves. Such ecosystems are being deployed under diverse and sometimes contradictory governance frameworks - from government-regulated EU Digital Identity Wallets to community-governed decentralized identity ecosystems based on self-sovereign identity principles. Designing these ecosystems requires satisfying constraints that span multiple abstraction layers - from domain-level claim properties through credential schema structuring to format-specific privacy capabilities - yet these constraints are currently scattered across sources such as W3C standards, EU implementing regulations, or community design guidelines, with no formal means to check their consistency or detect design errors.  
We present a layered metamodel and formalized constraint set for credential ecosystem design, spanning domain-level claim properties, abstract credential schemas, and format-specific credential representations. The metamodel is grounded in the W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0; cross-layer constraints are formalized as graph predicates in the Refinery partial graph modeling framework. We validate metamodel coverage against the W3C specification, constraint expressiveness against requirements from W3C and EU regulatory sources, and error detection against known credential design anti-patterns. Our results show that constraints originating from different governance frameworks can be formally conflicting, and that design errors spanning multiple layers - undetectable by single-layer inspection — become visible through the integrated formalization.
